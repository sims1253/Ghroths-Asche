
# GM-Friendly Adventure/Campaign Presentation Guide

**Core Philosophy:** Prioritize clarity, scanability, and ease of use *during play*. Structure information logically so the GM can find what they need *immediately*. Use formatting as a tool.

## I. Overall Document Structure

*   **Front Matter:**
    *   **Title Page:** Clear Title.
    *   **Table of Contents:** Clickable if digital. Essential for navigation.
    *   **Adventure Summary/Pitch:** Brief overview, core concept, expected playtime, player count, themes, content warnings.
    *   **Cast of Characters (Dramatis Personae):** Quick list of key NPCs with one-sentence descriptions/roles. Maybe page references.
    *   **Adventure Flowchart/Node Map (Optional but Recommended):** Visual overview of key locations/events and how Blocker clues connect them. Excellent for non-linear adventures.
    *   **How to Use This Adventure:** Briefly explain layout conventions, abbreviations, specific mechanics (like Clue Tiers).
*   **Main Body (Adventure Content):** Organized by scene, location, or node (see Section II).
*   **Appendices:**
    *   **NPC Stat Blocks:** Gather full stats here for easy reference, potentially with brief personality reminders.
    *   **Handouts:** All player-facing handouts, clearly numbered/labeled.
    *   **New Rules/Mechanics:** Any custom monsters, items, or rules variations.
    *   **Clue Summary/Checklist:** A table listing all Blocker/Background clues, where they can be found, and what they point towards. (Invaluable!)
    *   **Maps:** Player-facing and GM-only versions.

## II. Scene / Location / Node Layout (The Building Blocks)

**Goal:** Make each section self-contained enough to run, but clearly link outwards.

*   **Clear Heading:** (e.g., `## Scene 3: The Dilapidated Chapel` or `### Location: West Wing Study`)
*   **Overview/Entry:**
    *   **Read-Aloud Text (Optional & Concise):** Use sparingly in a distinct format (box or italics). Focus on initial sensory details and atmosphere. *Often better to provide bullet points for the GM to paraphrase.*
    *   **GM Summary:** 1-2 sentences explaining the location's purpose, current state, and main points of interest/conflict *for the GM*.
*   **Key Features / Contents:**
    *   **Bullet Points:** List significant objects, areas, environmental details. Use **bold** for interactable items or things likely to be examined.
    *   *Example:*
        *   Overturned oak **desk**, papers scattered.
        *   Stained-glass window depicting a **tentacled saint**, partially shattered.
        *   Faint **smell** of ozone and brine.
*   **Clues Present:**
    *   Use subheadings or distinct bullet points for different clues.
    *   Clearly label clue type (**Blocker**, **Background**, **Vibe**).
    *   State *how* the clue might be found (e.g., "Searching the desk [Blocker]", "Examining the window [Vibe]", "Successful `History` roll on the mural [Background]").
    *   State *what* the clue reveals or points to.
    *   *Example:*
        *   **Clues:**
            *   **[Blocker]** Searching the **desk drawer** (requires `Lockpicking` DC 12 or forcing): Finds a coded ledger pointing to the Smuggler's Dock (See *Scene 5*).
            *   **[Background]** Successful `Occult` roll (DC 14) when examining the **stained-glass window**: Recognizes the saint as an obscure avatar of a Deep One entity.
            *   **[Vibe]** The constant **smell of brine** deepens the sense of unease.
*   **NPCs Present:**
    *   List NPCs currently here.
    *   Include a *brief* reminder of their goal/attitude in this scene.
    *   Reference their full stat block in the Appendix (e.g., "See *Cultist Acolyte, App. A*").
*   **Potential Events/Encounters:**
    *   Triggers (e.g., "If players disturb the altar...", "If the clock strikes midnight...").
    *   Brief description of the event/encounter. Reference stats as needed.
*   **Exits/Connections:** Clearly state where players can go next and what clues point there.

## III. Handling NPCs

*   **Dramatis Personae (Front Matter):** Quick reference.
*   **Appendix Stat Blocks:** Full mechanical details + concise summary of:
    *   **Role:** Their function in the story.
    *   **Goal/Motivation:** What do they want?
    *   **Key Knowledge:** What Blocker/Background clues do they possess? Under what circumstances might they reveal them?
    *   **Appearance & Mannerisms:** Brief, evocative details.
    *   **Reactivity:** Quick notes on how they react to common player approaches (friendly, hostile, suspicious).
*   **In-Scene References:** Keep brief, linking to the Appendix.

## IV. Handling Clues

*   **Tiered System (Recommended):** Label clues (Blocker, Background, Vibe).
*   **Explicit Discovery Methods:** State *how* clues are found (specific search, skill check, interaction). Avoid "guess the GM's thought."
*   **Clear Statement of Finding:** "Players find X, which reveals Y..."
*   **Cross-Reference:** Link clues to the locations/NPCs/events they point towards.
*   **Clue Summary (Appendix):** A master list is crucial for GM overview and ensuring redundancy (Three Clue Rule for Blockers).

## V. Visuals & Handouts

*   **Maps:** Clear, legible keys. Provide GM versions with secrets marked. Integrate references (e.g., "Area 3a on the Map").
*   **Handouts:** Numbered/labeled clearly. Referenced in the text where they are found. Ensure they look good and fit the tone (use appropriate fonts, distressing effects if digital).
*   **Layout & Art:** Use whitespace effectively. Break up text walls. Use art and layout to enhance mood, not just decorate. Consider iconography (Mothership style) for quick references.

## VI. Language & Tone

*   **Be Concise:** Use clear, direct language for GM instructions.
*   **Be Evocative:** Use stronger sensory language for descriptions meant to build atmosphere.
*   **Use Formatting:** **Bold** key terms. Use *italics* for emphasis or read-aloud (use sparingly). Use bullet points and lists liberally.
*   **Separate GM Info:** Use sidebars, distinct formatting (like indented text or different background color if digital), or clear "[GM NOTE:]" tags for secret information.

## VII. GM Aids within the Text

*   **Skill Check DCs:** Include difficulties directly in the text (e.g., `Perception` DC 15).
*   **Page References:** Cross-reference vigorously (to NPC stats, map locations, other scenes, rulebook pages if necessary).
*   **Countdown Clocks/Timelines:** If applicable, present clearly, perhaps in a sidebar or dedicated section.
*   **Pronunciation Guides:** For difficult Cthulhu-esque names.

## VIII. Campaign Considerations

*   **Overarching Elements:** Include sections summarizing campaign-level threats, recurring NPCs, and major plot arcs *separate* from individual adventures.
*   **Consequence Tracking:** Provide guidance or space for the GM to track long-term consequences (Sanity, Bonds, narrative changes).
*   **Interlude/Downtime:** Offer suggestions or frameworks for handling downtime between adventures if relevant to the system/campaign style.

---
**Final Self-Check:** Before finalizing, read through a section pretending you have distracted players asking unexpected questions. Can you *quickly* find the relevant rule, NPC motive, or location detail? If not, improve the formatting and clarity.
